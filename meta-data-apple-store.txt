APP NAME
Orbit Recall

SUBTITLE
Space memory drills, offline

KEYWORDS

PROMOTION
Build missions of space facts, anchor each one to a picture you can't forget, then drill your weakest cues first. Grade yourself and watch the ring fill. Private, offline.

DESCRIPTION
Orbit Recall is an offline memory-training deck for space and science class.

Everything starts with a mission — one topic you actually need to know. The eight planets. Jupiter's moons. The Apollo flights. Give it a code, pick a track, and set how many cues you are aiming for.

Inside a mission you file cues. The front of a cue is what you get asked; the anchor is the strange picture that answers it — a rusty red bicycle in the hallway for Mars, a hula hoop on the kitchen table for Saturn. Add a photo, tag it, and it is ready to train.

The drill room queues your weakest cues first: faded, then shaky, then fresh. Flip a card, say the answer out loud, and grade yourself honestly — Missed, Shaky, Got it, or Locked. Every grade updates the cue's recall level, bumps its rep count, and feeds the progress rings on your deck.

WHAT YOU GET

• Missions grouped by track — planets, moons, deep sky, space tech, physics, numbers, timeline
• Cues with an anchor, a photo, tags and twelve cue types (peg, journey, story, image, number, acronym and more)
• A drill room with weakest-first or starred-only runs, in blocks of 5, 10, 20 or 40
• Five recall levels — Fresh, Shaky, Steady, Locked, Faded — with a run score at the end of every session
• A starred drill deck for the cues you want in tonight's run
• Progress page with recall spread, breakdown by track and by cue type, and total reps
• Flight log recording every time a cue is reassigned to another mission, with your note
• Search across cue fronts, anchors, tags and types
• Light and dark appearance, or match your phone
• Export everything to a single JSON file and restore it whenever you want

PRIVATE BY DESIGN

There is no account and no sign-in. Orbit Recall makes no network calls at all — missions, cues, photos and drill history live in this app's own storage on your phone. Camera and photo library access are optional and used only when you attach a picture to a cue.

Good for students revising for a science test, teachers building a class set of cues, and anyone who wants their astronomy facts to stick.
